Privacy Policy — Catch

Last updated 9 September 2026

Catch collects no data about you. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server of its own. Your catches, your photos and your fishing spots are stored on your device and nowhere else.

The one thing that leaves your phone

Catch makes exactly one kind of network request, and it is worth being precise about it rather than claiming the app never goes online.

When you save a catch with Record conditions switched on, Catch sends that catch's coordinate and time to Apple's WeatherKit service to look up the weather at that place and moment — temperature, air pressure, wind and conditions. That result is stored with your catch on your device. The request goes to Apple and nowhere else. It carries no account, no name and no identifier for you, because the app has none to send.

Apple's handling of WeatherKit requests is governed by Apple's own privacy policy, not by this one. The developer of Catch receives nothing back from Apple except the weather, and has no way to see which requests came from you.

You can turn this off. Settings → Record conditions stops it entirely, and with it off Catch makes no network requests at all. Catches you save then have no weather attached; everything else works exactly the same, and moon phase still appears because it is calculated on your device from the date rather than fetched.

Your fishing spots

This is the part most anglers care about, so it is stated plainly.

  • When you save a catch, Catch records where you were, using your device's location. That position is stored on your device only.
  • Exact positions are never included in a CSV export or a shared catch unless you switch them on for that specific action, and that switch resets to off every single time. There is no setting anywhere that makes sharing your positions the default.
  • Nobody else can see your spots. There is no feed, no community map and no account, so there is no mechanism by which one angler's positions could reach another.
  • Location is requested only while you are using the app. Catch does no background location tracking, no significant-change monitoring and no geofencing.
  • If you decline location access, Catch still works. Catches simply save without a position, and you can name the water yourself.

What Catch stores on your device

The catches you log — species, weight, length, the water's name, the date and time, the coordinate, the conditions, any bait or note you typed, and any photo you attached. All of it lives in the app's own storage on your iPhone.

Photos

A photo you attach to a catch is copied into the app's storage on your device. It is not uploaded anywhere. Catch asks for photo library or camera access only at the moment you choose to add one.

What Catch does not do

  • No account or sign-in, so there is nothing to identify you by.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party SDKs or services of any kind.
  • No social feed, profile or sharing of your catches with other users.
  • No data is sold or shared, because none is collected.

Backups

If you have iPhone backups switched on, your Catch data is included in your own device backup, the same as any other app's. That backup is between you, your device and Apple; the developer of Catch has no access to it and receives nothing from it.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes all of its data from your device. There is no server-side copy to request the deletion of, and no account to close. If you want to keep your log first, export it as CSV from Settings.

Children

Catch is suitable for general audiences and collects nothing from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Catch ever collects anything, or adds a second network service, this policy will be updated before that version is released and the change will be described in the app's release notes.
